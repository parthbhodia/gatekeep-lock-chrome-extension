Cat Break vs Freedom

These two barely overlap, and pretending otherwise would waste your time. Freedom is a paid, cross-platform blocker that covers your phone, your laptop and your tablet together. Cat Break is a free Chrome extension that nudges you in one browser. The right choice comes down to one question: where does your problem actually happen?

Pick Freedom if…

Your scrolling moves between devices. You put the laptop down and pick the phone up. Freedom blocks across Mac, Windows, iOS, Android and Chrome at once, with scheduled and recurring sessions and a locked mode you can't casually switch off. Cat Break cannot touch your phone, and no browser extension can.

Pick Cat Break if…

It happens in one browser, on one machine, and you'd rather not pay a subscription or create an account to fix it. Cat Break is free, local, and asks for nothing.

Be honest about the phone

This is the whole decision, so it's worth being blunt. If you close the laptop and reach for your phone, a Chrome extension has solved nothing — it has just moved the problem to a screen it can't see. Cat Break runs inside Chrome. It has no idea your phone exists. Freedom does, and that is a real and substantial advantage that no amount of free-and-open-source makes up for.

If the phone is the problem, buy the thing that covers the phone. We'd rather tell you that than sell you a half-fix.

If, on the other hand, the damage is done at a desk — the tab you keep flicking back to while working, the hour that disappears between tasks — then a browser-level nudge is aimed at exactly the right place, and you don't need a subscription for it.

Cost and commitment

Freedom has a limited free tier and a paid subscription that unlocks scheduling, locked mode and unlimited sessions; there are monthly, annual and lifetime options, and the current numbers are on their site. It needs an account, because syncing blocks across your devices requires a server that knows who you are. That is a reasonable trade for what it does, not a criticism.

Cat Break has no paid tier to upgrade to, no account, and no server. Your settings and daily totals live in chrome.storage on your own machine. The only network request the extension makes is downloading the cat video.

Using both

They coexist fine, and the combination is genuinely reasonable: Freedom for scheduled deep-work sessions and phone coverage, Cat Break for the ordinary weekday hours when you don't want to be locked out of anything but would like someone to tap you on the shoulder after 45 minutes.

The honest summary

Freedom does more, across more devices, and charges for it. Cat Break does one thing in one browser and doesn't. Neither of those is a flaw — they are answers to different questions. Work out whether your problem is multi-device or single-browser first, and the choice makes itself.

Questions

Is Cat Break a free alternative to Freedom?

Only partly. Cat Break is free and needs no account, but it runs in Chrome only. It cannot block your phone, other browsers, or desktop apps, all of which Freedom does.

Does Cat Break need a subscription?

No. It is free with no paid tier, no ads and no account, and it is MIT licensed with the source public on GitHub.

Can I use Cat Break and Freedom together?

Yes. They work at different levels and don't conflict.

Does Cat Break send my browsing anywhere?

No. Settings and daily totals stay in chrome.storage on your machine. The only request it makes is fetching the cat video.
