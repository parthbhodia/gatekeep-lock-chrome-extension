How to limit your YouTube time in Chrome

YouTube is the hardest site to time-limit, for two reasons most tools handle badly: it plays in background tabs, and the next video starts by itself. Here are the options that exist, and what each one gets right.

Option 1: YouTube's own reminder

YouTube has a built-in “Take a break” reminder, and it costs nothing to switch on. In YouTube's settings, under General, you'll find reminder options plus a bedtime reminder.

It's the lowest-effort option and worth enabling regardless. The limitation is that it's a dismissable overlay inside a site whose whole business is keeping you there, it only covers YouTube, and it doesn't stop playback. Most people click through it without registering that they did.

Option 2: A per-site limit in a Chrome extension

This is the approach that fits YouTube best, because you can give it a different allowance from everything else — 45 minutes for YouTube while leaving your docs and mail untouched. Any of the extensions in this category can do it; they differ in what happens when you hit the limit, which is covered below.

Option 3: Block it outright during certain hours

If YouTube has no legitimate place in your working day, a scheduled block is simpler than a time limit. Tools like LeechBlock NG and StayFocusd support time-of-day rules, and Cold Turkey enforces them across every browser at once.

Option 4: Cut the recommendations instead of the time

Worth knowing because it addresses a different failure. Plenty of people don't overrun on the video they came for — they overrun on the six the sidebar suggested afterwards. Extensions exist that hide the homepage feed, the sidebar and Shorts, leaving search and subscriptions working. If you arrive with a purpose and leave with none, that may be a better fix than a timer.

The two things most tools get wrong

Background tabs

Leave a YouTube tab open behind your work and many trackers keep counting it. You get told you spent three hours on YouTube when you spent forty minutes, the number is obviously wrong, and you stop trusting the tool. Look for something that counts only the time a tab is focused — that's the difference between a number you act on and a number you dismiss.

Autoplay

A reminder that appears while the video keeps playing isn't a break. YouTube's player is also awkward to control from an extension because it sits inside shadow DOM, so plenty of tools simply don't try. If playback doesn't stop, you'll read the message and keep watching.

If it doesn't pause the video, it isn't a break. It's a subtitle.

Setting a YouTube limit with Cat Break

Cat Break is a free Chrome extension built around both of those problems. To set a YouTube-specific limit:

  • Install it from the Chrome Web Store and open the popup from your toolbar.
  • Set a default limit — anything from 15 minutes up to 600.
  • Under Limit by site, add youtube.com with its own number. A per-site limit overrides the default.
  • Optionally add the sites you never want timed, like your mail or your docs, under Excluded sites. Or turn on list-only mode so nothing except the domains you named is timed at all.
  • Choose how long the break lasts. It clears itself when the countdown ends.

When you cross the limit a cat walks onto the page and the video pauses — including YouTube's shadow-DOM player, and if a page refuses to pause, the tab is muted instead. Playback resumes afterwards. Background tabs never count against you, because the timer only runs while the tab is focused. Press Esc if you genuinely need to keep going; it's a nudge, not a lock.

Nothing about this leaves your machine: no account, no server, no sync. The only network request the extension makes is fetching the cat video itself.

Which to choose

Turn on YouTube's own reminder today — it's free and takes a minute. If that isn't enough, add a per-site limit in an extension. If you route around that too, move up to scheduled blocking or system-level enforcement. Start light; you'll learn quickly which level you actually need.

Questions

Does YouTube have a built-in time limit?

It has reminder features rather than a hard limit: a 'Take a break' reminder and a bedtime reminder in settings. They prompt you but do not block access or stop playback.

Can I limit YouTube without blocking it completely?

Yes. A per-site time limit lets you use YouTube normally until you reach your allowance for the day, rather than blocking it outright.

Do background YouTube tabs count towards my limit in Cat Break?

No. The timer only runs while the tab is the active, focused one, so a YouTube tab left open behind your work does not consume your allowance.

Will an extension pause the video when the limit is reached?

Cat Break does, including YouTube's shadow-DOM player, and mutes the tab if a page refuses to pause. Many tools do not, which is worth checking before you rely on one.
